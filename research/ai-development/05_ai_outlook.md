# 05. Future Outlook

> This section combines trends observed in the sources with reasoned projections. Projections are not certainties.

## 1. From Tools to Agents

- AI is moving from answering prompts to carrying out multi-step work on its own, as specialized multi-agent teams and long-running "digital colleagues."
- Human roles shift toward setting goals, supervising, and verifying AI output.

## 2. Continued Capability Growth, but Uneven

- Benchmarks are saturating quickly (e.g., SWE-bench from ~60% to near 100% in one year), so new evaluation methods based on real work will matter more.
- The "jagged frontier" means AI will stay superhuman at some tasks and unreliable at others; human judgment remains necessary.

## 3. Infrastructure and Energy Constraints

- Data-center electricity demand is expected to more than double by 2030 (IEA), with AI-server demand roughly tripling.
- Power supply, chips, and grid capacity may become the main limits on AI growth; energy efficiency and clean power become strategic issues.

## 4. Global Competition

- The US–China performance gap has closed, while the US still dominates investment, startups, and data centers.
- Talent mobility is changing: AI researchers moving to the US fell 89% since 2017 (Stanford AI Index 2026).

## 5. Governance Catching Up

- 2026 is a key year for regulation: Korea's AI Basic Act (Jan 2026) and the bulk of the EU AI Act (Aug 2026) take effect.
- The challenge is balancing innovation with safety while AI incidents keep rising.

## 6. Workforce Transition

- Net job creation is projected (WEF: +78M by 2030), but large-scale churn and reskilling (59% of workers) are needed.
- AI literacy in schools and lifelong learning become essential; education currently lags (only half of US middle/high schools have AI policies).
