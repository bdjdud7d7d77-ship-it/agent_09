# 02. Latest AI Technology Trends (2025–2026)

## 1. Capabilities Are Accelerating, Not Plateauing

According to the Stanford HAI **2026 AI Index Report**:
- On the coding benchmark **SWE-bench Verified**, top scores rose from about 60% to near 100% in a single year.
- Several models now meet or exceed human baselines on PhD-level science questions, multimodal reasoning, and competition mathematics.
- Industry produced **over 90%** of notable frontier models in 2025.
- **US–China gap has effectively closed**: the lead changed hands several times since early 2025; as of March 2026 the top US model (Anthropic) led by only 2.7%.

## 2. Reasoning Models

- Models such as OpenAI o1, DeepSeek-R1, Claude with extended thinking, and Gemini "think through" problems before answering.
- They spend extra compute at inference time ("test-time compute") on multi-step deliberation, raising accuracy on math, science, and coding.

## 3. Multimodal AI

- Single models understand and generate text, images, audio, and video.
- Different media are encoded into a shared representation so the model can reason across them (e.g., watch a video, listen to machine sounds, and read a manual to diagnose a fault).
- Multimodal capabilities are now built into the core APIs of major cloud providers.

## 4. AI Agents and Multi-Agent Systems

- The shift is from **passive tools that respond** to **autonomous agents that act**: planning, using tools, browsing, writing and running code.
- Instead of one "super-agent," teams of specialized agents (Multi-Agent Systems) collaborate on complex tasks.
- Agents are moving from chatbots toward "digital colleagues" that keep working over long periods.

## 5. The "Jagged Frontier"

- AI ability is uneven: the same models that win gold at the International Mathematical Olympiad read analog clocks correctly only about 50% of the time.
- Headline benchmark scores are a poor guide to how a model performs on a specific real-world task.
